Mosquito-borne infectious diseases, such as dengue fever and Chikungunya, are experiencing rapid and sustained increases in both geographic prevalence and epidemic scale, posing a significant and growing threat to global public health systems. From manual specimen collection to the development of traps in 1928 and the recent emergence of automated monitoring tools, interdisciplinary collaboration has served as the central driver of technological advancement. In the current era, propelled by innovations in perception technology, artificial intelligence (AI), Internet of Things (IoTs), and drones, mosquito surveillance is transitioning from traditional approaches toward intelligent, integrated systems. This evolution holds great promise for establishing efficient, reliable, and precise surveillance networks, thereby advancing the sustainable control of mosquito-borne diseases on a global scale.
